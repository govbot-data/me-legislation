18 1. Investigation. Whenever the director believes a person or employer has violated
19 any provision of chapter 7 or 15, the director may conduct an investigation. During the
20 investigation of a person or employer, the director may:
21 A. Examine or cause to be examined for that purpose any books, records, papers and
22 memoranda of any person or employer of whatever nature relevant to the alleged
23 violation;
24 B. Issue a subpoena pursuant to subsection 2 or otherwise require the attendance of
25 the person or employer, or of any other person having knowledge of the subject of the
26 investigation, take testimony, administer oaths or take acknowledgement in respect of
27 any book, record, paper or memorandum and may require proof material for that
28 person's or employer's information. Such testimony, administration of oaths or
29 acknowledgement of any book, record, paper or memorandum must be taken in the
30 county where the person or employer resides or has a place of business, or in Kennebec
31 County if the person or employer is a nonresident or has no place of business within
32 the State; and
33 C. Require production of any record required by law to be kept by the person or
34 employer pursuant to any provision of chapter 7 or 15.

14 3. Penalties for violations. If the director determines a person or employer has
15 violated any provision of chapter 7 or 15, the director may issue a notice of violation and:
16 A. Order the payment of penalties and other remedies consistent with section 53 and
17 any other penalty or enforcement provision in chapter 7 or 15. Once a penalty or other
18 ordered remedy is final and all administrative and legal appeal rights have been
19 exhausted, the person or employer subject to the penalty or ordered remedy pursuant
20 to this subsection shall make payment within 30 calendar days. Failure to make a timely
21 payment is subject to an additional civil penalty of not more than $1,000 for each day
22 that the person or employer fails to make payment; or
23 B. Order the correction of ongoing violations. Any person or employer who fails to
24 correct a violation for which a notice of violation has been issued within the permitted
25 period for its correction is subject to an additional civil penalty of not more than $1,000
26 for each day during which the violation is not corrected. The permitted period for
27 correction may not begin to run until the date on which all administrative and legal
28 appeal rights have been exhausted in the case of any appeal proceeding initiated by the
29 person or employer in good faith and not solely for delay or avoidance of penalties.
30 A person or employer who is ordered to pay wages, liquidated damages or interest to an
31 employee pursuant to this subsection or the provisions of chapter 7 or 15 shall certify that
32 payment was made within 7 calendar days from the date on which all administrative and
33 legal appeal rights have been exhausted in the case of any appeal proceeding initiated by
34 the person or employer in good faith and not solely for delay or avoidance of penalties.
35 Such certification must demonstrate to the satisfaction of the director that payment was
36 made to any employee's last known address or attest to the same under penalty of perjury.

1 SUMMARY
22 This amendment replaces the bill and changes the title. The amendment provides the
33 Director of Labor Standards within the Department of Labor the authority to conduct an
44 investigation when the director believes a person or employer has violated any provision
55 of the Maine Revised Statutes, Title 26, chapter 7 or 15. The amendment also provides the
66 penalties the director may order if it is determined that a violation has occurred and
77 authorizes the director to issue a notice of levy if the person or employer charged with a
88 violation fails to pay. The amendment also requires persons or employers to prominently
99 post in the workplace the notices of violation issued for a violation and requires the person
1100 or employer to notify employees if a notice of violation covers a defined period of time.
