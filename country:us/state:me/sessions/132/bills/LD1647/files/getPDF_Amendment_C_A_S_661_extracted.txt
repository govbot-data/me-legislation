9 COMMITTEE AMENDMENT “ ” to S.P. 655, L.D. 1647, “An Act to Amend the
10 Maine Human Rights Act to Provide Additional Remedies for Educational Discrimination”
11 Amend the bill by striking out all of section 2 and inserting the following:
12 'Sec. 2. 5 MRSA §4613, sub-§2, ¶B, as amended by PL 2023, c. 263, §1, is further
13 amended by enacting a new subparagraph (7-A) to read:
14 (7-A) In cases of intentional educational discrimination, compensatory damages
15 as provided in this subparagraph.
16 (a) A complaining party may recover compensatory damages for loss of
17 education, loss of educational opportunity, past and future pecuniary losses,
18 emotional pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental anguish, loss of enjoyment
19 of life and other nonpecuniary losses.
20 (b) The sum of compensatory damages awarded for intentional educational
21 discrimination may not exceed $100,000 for each complaining party.
22 (c) Compensatory damages may not be awarded under this subparagraph
23 unless the complaining party provides actual notice to the educational
24 institution.
25 (d) Compensatory damages awarded under this subparagraph do not include
26 any other type of relief authorized elsewhere under this subsection.
27 (e) This subparagraph may not be construed to limit the scope of or the relief
28 available under any other state or federal law.
29 (f) If a complaining party seeks compensatory damages under this
30 subparagraph, any party may demand a trial by jury.
31 (g) This subparagraph does not apply to recoveries for a practice that is
32 unlawful under this Title only because of its disparate impact;

3 SUMMARY
44 This amendment limits to $100,000 the amount a complaining party may recover in
55 compensatory damages under the bill in cases of intentional educational discrimination
66 under the Maine Human Rights Act. The amendment also specifies that compensatory
77 damages may not be awarded unless the complaining party provides actual notice to the
88 educational institution that is alleged to have committed the educational discrimination.
99 The amendment also removes the provision of the bill that provides that these
1100 compensatory damages are not available in cases when the educational institution has
1111 demonstrated good faith efforts to make a reasonable accommodation.
1122 The amendment also provides funding to the Maine Community College System to
1133 establish one Assistant General Counsel position to handle cases related to educational
1144 discrimination and to the University of Maine System to establish one Investigator position
1155 in the Office of Equal Opportunity and Title IX Services to support investigations related
1166 to educational discrimination.
