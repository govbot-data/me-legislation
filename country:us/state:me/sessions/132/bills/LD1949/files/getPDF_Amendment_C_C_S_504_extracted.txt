13 'Sec. 1. 35-A MRSA §103-B is enacted to read:
14 §103-B. Affordability impact considerations
15 In executing its duties, powers and regulatory functions under this Title, the
16 commission, while ensuring system reliability, shall consider the impact on affordability
17 for residential customers.
18 Sec. 2. Credit and collection data; publication. The Public Utilities
19 Commission shall publish on its publicly accessible website data regarding credit and
20 collection activities of a transmission and distribution utility with more than 50,000
21 customers that is submitted to the commission by the utility in accordance with rules
22 adopted by the commission pursuant to the Maine Revised Statutes, Title 35-A, section
23 704, subsection 1. The commission shall ensure that the data is provided on the
24 commission's publicly accessible website in a clear and transparent manner.
25 Sec. 3. Comprehensive review of electric delivery rates. The Public Utilities
26 Commission shall conduct a comprehensive review of each component of electric delivery
27 rates. In conducting the review required pursuant to this section, the commission shall
28 consider whether changes to the current design and composition of electric delivery rates
29 could be implemented to, at a minimum:
30 1. Contain customer costs;
31 2. Reduce transmission and distribution utility bill volatility; and
32 3. Increase transmission and distribution utility bill transparency.
33 The commission shall engage stakeholders in the review process under this section and
34 solicit stakeholder feedback. By January 31, 2027, the commission shall submit an interim

20 SUMMARY
2211 This amendment replaces the bill. The amendment:
22 1. Requires the Public Utilities Commission, in executing its duties, powers and
23 regulatory functions under the Maine Revised Statutes, Title 35-A, while ensuring system
24 reliability, to consider the impact on affordability for residential customers;
25 2. Requires the commission to publish on its publicly accessible website data regarding
26 credit and collection activities of a transmission and distribution utility with more than
27 50,000 customers that is submitted to the commission by the utility in accordance with
28 commission rules. The commission must ensure that the data is provided in a clear and
29 transparent manner;
30 3. Requires the commission to conduct a comprehensive review of each component of
31 electric delivery rates. In conducting the review, the commission must consider, at a
32 minimum, options that aim to contain customer costs in electric delivery rates, reduce
33 transmission and distribution utility bill volatility and increase transmission and
34 distribution utility bill transparency; and
35 4. Requires the commission to develop an affordability metric to be used to assess the
36 impact of electricity bills on the overall energy burden for residential customers of an
37 investor-owned transmission and distribution utility. By January 15, 2027, the commission
38 must submit an interim report to the joint standing committee of the Legislature having
39 jurisdiction over energy matters and submit a final report to the committee by December
40 15, 2027.
