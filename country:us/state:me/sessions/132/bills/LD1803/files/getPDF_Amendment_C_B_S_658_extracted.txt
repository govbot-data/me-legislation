21 1. Definitions. For the purposes of this section, unless the context otherwise indicates,

23 A. "Diagnostic and therapeutic pharmaceutical agent" means any prescription or
24 nonprescription drug delivered by any route of administration, which may be used or

30 which in vivo tissue is injected, cut, burned, frozen, sutured, vaporized, coagulated or
31 photo disrupted by the use of surgical instrumentation, including, but not limited to, a
32 scalpel, cryoprobe, laser or electric cautery, or by the use of ionizing radiation.
33 "Ophthalmic surgery" does not include any surgical procedures that do not involve the
34 eye or its adnexa.

1 2. Practice of optometry. The practice of optometry is the evaluation, diagnosis,
2 prevention or treatment of diseases, disorders or conditions of the human vision system,
3 eyes and adjacent and associated structures, including, in addition to all forms of care and
4 treatment approved on the effective date of this subsection:
5 A. Measuring the powers and range of vision of the human eye using subjective and
6 objective means, including the use of lenses, prisms and automated testing devices, to
7 determine its accommodative and refractive state and general scope of function of
8 human vision;
9 B. The adaptation, sale and dispensing of frames and lenses in all their forms to
10 overcome errors of refraction and restore as near as possible normal human vision;
11 C. The adaptation, sale and dispensing of plano or zero power contact lenses;
12 D. Ordering appropriate diagnostic laboratory or imaging tests for the purpose of
13 prescribing contact lenses for prosthetic or therapeutic purposes, including
14 orthokeratology;
15 E. Ordering appropriate diagnostic laboratory or imaging tests to facilitate the
16 provision of contact lenses for cosmetic purposes;
17 F. The dispensing of samples to initiate treatment;
18 G. The use or prescription of lenses, prisms, vision therapy and vision rehabilitation;
19 H. The prescription of diagnostic and therapeutic pharmaceutical agents for ocular
20 disease, by any delivery system necessary, including controlled substances included in
21 schedules III, IV and V as described in 21 United States Code, Section 812;
22 I. The use and prescription of medical devices;
23 J. The prescription of oral and topical antihistamines or anti-allergy medication,
24 including the prescription of medicated contact lenses;
25 K. The prescription of topical anti-inflammatory medication and oral nonsteroidal anti-
26 inflammatories and corticosteroids;
27 L. The prescription of oral and topical anti-glaucoma medications, including oral
28 acetazolamide;
29 M. The use of therapeutic ultrasound, radio frequency and intense pulsed light
30 treatments; and
31 N. Ophthalmic surgery as expressly provided in this paragraph:
32 (1) The intradermal injection of a neuromuscular blocking agent into the eyelids;
33 and
34 (2) The independent performance of corneal collagen cross-linking involving an
35 intact corneal epithelium.
36 Notwithstanding any provision of this chapter to the contrary, a person may not
37 perform ophthalmic surgery in accordance with this paragraph until the board has
38 finally adopted rules in accordance with subsection 4 and sections 19103 and 19204.

1 SUMMARY
22 This amendment, which is the minority report of the committee, replaces the bill. The
33 amendment does the following.
4 The amendment makes changes to the provisions of the law governing optometrists by
5 providing a more detailed explanation of what constitutes the practice of optometry. The
6 definition of "practice of optometry" in current law does not include surgical procedures.
7 The amendment, which is more limited in scope than the majority report, includes only 2
8 specific ophthalmic surgeries in the practice of optometry and all other procedures are
9 specifically excluded. The amendment directs the State Board of Optometry to adopt major
10 substantive rules, subject to legislative approval before final adoption, to further define the
11 scope of practice of optometry and establish credentialing requirements. An optometrist
12 may not perform ophthalmic surgery as provided in the amendment until the board has
13 finally approved major substantive rules regarding an optometrist's scope of practice and
14 credentialing standards.
15 The amendment also broadens the authority of an optometrist to dispense and
16 administer certain medications independently and removes language that prohibits an
17 optometrist from administering drugs by injection or intravenously. The amendment
18 authorizes the board to issue advisory opinions and declaratory rulings. The amendment
19 requires an optometrist to obtain malpractice insurance in an amount established by the
20 board and also makes minor changes to the provisions relating to telehealth services and
21 filling contact lenses and spectacle prescriptions.
22 The amendment also establishes the Task Force to Study the Optometry Practice Laws
23 and Recommend Changes to Modernize Board Oversight and to Update the Licensure and
24 Scope of Practice Provisions and directs the task force to study the Maine Revised Statutes,
25 Title 32, chapter 151, which governs optometrists, and any laws associated with the
26 provisions of that chapter to recommend structural and administrative changes to
27 modernize board oversight and changes to update the licensure and scope of practice
28 provisions for optometrists to more accurately reflect their education and training and the
29 practice settings and delivery models for optometry services in this State. The task force is
30 required to report any recommendations from the study to the joint standing committee of
31 the Legislature having jurisdiction over matters concerning the practice of optometry on or
32 before February 1, 2027. The joint standing committee may report out a bill to the 133rd
33 Legislature in 2027 related only to the task force’s recommendations to modernize board
34 oversight and to continue the task force process. The joint standing committee may report
35 out a bill to the Second Regular Session of the 133rd Legislature to update the licensure
36 and scope of practice provisions for optometrists.
