13 SUMMARY
1144 This amendment, which is the majority report of the committee, changes the title of the
1155 bill. The amendment removes the provisions of the bill directing the Office of the Attorney
1166 General to create a centralized complaint process and procedures for investigations of
1177 violations of crime victim rights.
18 Under current law, a crime victim may file a motion to quash any subpoena served on
19 that victim. The bill provides that a victim has a right to receive timely notice and an
20 opportunity to be heard when a party to a court proceeding requests a subpoena granting
21 that party access to certain types of records about or concerning the victim. The amendment
22 clarifies that the attorney for the State has responsibility for making a good faith effort to
23 notify the victim of the existence of a subpoena request; the date and time of any proceeding
24 related to the subpoena; and that the victim must be given the opportunity to be heard at
25 that proceeding or hearing when a party to a criminal proceeding seeks a subpoena from
26 someone other than the victim for any record that implicates a privilege or a confidentiality
27 or privacy protection for the benefit of the victim as described in Rule 17A(f) of the Maine
28 Rules of Unified Criminal Procedure, including, but not limited to, the victim's medical
29 records, mental health counseling or treatment records, educational records, electronic
30 device content information and electronic device location information.
31 The amendment also clarifies that, in any court proceeding in a criminal case in which
32 a victim has a right to be heard, the victim may retain an attorney to speak on the victim's
33 behalf or may elect to have a victim witness advocate or victim witness coordinator speak
34 on the victim's behalf. Treatment of the victim may not be affected in any way based on
35 the victim's decision to retain an attorney, except as required to comply with the Maine
36 Rules of Professional Conduct governing attorney communications with persons who are
37 represented by counsel.
