14 'Emergency preamble. Whereas, acts and resolves of the Legislature do not
15 become effective until 90 days after adjournment unless enacted as emergencies; and
16 Whereas, this Act extends the prohibitions in current state law against the creation,
17 dissemination and possession of sexually explicit material depicting minors engaged in
18 sexually explicit conduct to similarly prohibit the creation, dissemination and possession
19 of images that have been modified or generated, including through the use of artificial
20 intelligence, or AI, technology, so that the images appear to depict minors engaged in
21 sexually explicit conduct; and
22 Whereas, perpetrators of these crimes have already begun creating child sexual abuse
23 material using technology through which they modify existing images of identifiable
24 children or through which they wholly create images that appear to depict children engaged
25 in sexually explicit conduct, but these perpetrators cannot be prosecuted because this
26 conduct is not currently prohibited by state law; and
27 Whereas, as generative artificial intelligence technology continues to advance, it is
28 becoming more and more difficult to distinguish between unaltered child sexual abuse
29 material subject to prosecution under current law and modified or AI-generated child
30 sexual abuse material not currently subject to prosecution; and
31 Whereas, it is essential to clarify state law as soon as possible to prevent sexual
32 exploitation of children; and
33 Whereas, in the judgment of the Legislature, these facts create an emergency within
34 the meaning of the Constitution of Maine and require the following legislation as

15 SUMMARY
1166 This amendment replaces the bill, which is a concept draft. The amendment makes
1177 several changes to chapter 12 within the Maine Criminal Code, which prohibits the
1188 creation, possession and dissemination of sexually explicit material, referred to in the
1199 amendment as child sexual abuse material. These changes are intended to protect children
2200 from offenses involving child sexual abuse material that are facilitated by technology.
21 The amendment defines the terms "child sexual abuse material," "identifiable child,"
22 "image," "obscene material" and "generative AI or machine learning" for purposes of
23 chapter 12 of the Maine Criminal Code. It also amends the definition of "minor" for
24 purposes of the chapter to include only a person who is less than 16 years of age, except
25 that, if the crime of sexual exploitation of a minor involves compulsion, the definition of
26 "minor" continues to include any person who is less than 18 years of age.
27 The amendment renames the crime of "dissemination of sexually explicit material" to
28 the crime of "dissemination of child sexual abuse material" and similarly renames the crime
29 of "possession of sexually explicit material" to the crime of "possession of child sexual
30 abuse material." It also expands the elements of these crimes as well as the crime of sexual
31 exploitation of a minor, which involves the creation of child sexual abuse material, to apply
32 both:
33 1. To child sexual abuse material that depicts or that has been created or modified so
34 that it appears to depict an identifiable child engaged in sexually explicit conduct; and
35 2. To child sexual abuse material, including an image created through generative AI
36 or machine learning, that appears to depict a minor engaged in sexually explicit conduct,
37 regardless of whether the minor is an identifiable child, if that child sexual abuse material
38 is obscene material.
39 The amendment provides that, for the purposes of any of the crimes set forth in chapter
40 12 of the Maine Criminal Code, the age of a person who is depicted or who appears to be
41 depicted in an image and the fact that the person is an identifiable child may be reasonably
42 inferred from the contents of the image or may be established through competent medical
