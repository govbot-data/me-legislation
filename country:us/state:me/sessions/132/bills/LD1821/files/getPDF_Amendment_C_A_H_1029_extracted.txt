16 §501. Short title
17 This chapter may be known and cited as "the Firearm Dealer Responsible Business

19 §502. Definitions
20 As used in this chapter, unless the context otherwise indicates, the following terms
21 have the following meanings.
22 1. ATF. "ATF" means the federal Department of Justice, Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco,
23 Firearms and Explosives or its successor agency.
24 2. Department. "Department" means the Department of Public Safety.
25 3. Federally licensed firearms dealer. "Federally licensed firearms dealer" has the
26 same meaning as in section 395, subsection 1, paragraph E.
27 4. Firearm. "Firearm" has the same meaning as in Title 17-A, section 2, subsection
28 12-A.
29 5. Gun show. "Gun show" has the same meaning as in section 395, subsection 1,
30 paragraph G.

12 §503. Security
13 A federally licensed firearms dealer shall take reasonable steps to prevent loss or theft
14 of firearms by adopting the following minimum security measures:
15 1. Business or firearms unattended. Whenever the place of business or firearms are
16 unattended, ensuring that the place of business is secure through the use of controls such
17 as door locks, window locks, alarms and window bars or ensuring that firearms are secure
18 through the use of a safe or other locking device; and
19 2. Other security measures. Any other reasonable and effective security measures
20 designed to reduce the risk of theft.
21 §504. Employee vetting and training
22 1. Background checks on employees required. A federally licensed firearms dealer
23 may not employ a person to handle, sell or deliver firearms unless the person is at least 18
24 years of age and is not prohibited from possessing a firearm under state or federal law as
25 determined by a background check using a background check entity authorized by the
26 department. This background check must be completed every 2 years.
27 2. Approved federally licensed firearms dealer employee training. The department
28 shall provide on its publicly accessible website a list of approved employee training courses
29 and materials for use by federally licensed firearms dealers. The approved training courses
30 must address, at a minimum, the following topics:
31 A. Federal and state laws governing firearm transactions;
32 B. How to recognize, identify, respond to and report straw purchases, illegal purchases
33 and fraudulent activity;
34 C. How to recognize, identify, respond to and report individuals who intend to use
35 firearms for unlawful purposes, including self-harm;
36 D. How to prevent, respond to and report theft or burglary of firearms and ammunition;
37 E. How to educate customers on gun safety practices, including but not limited to the
38 safe handling and storage of firearms and ammunition; and
39 F. Any other responsible business practice identified by the department.
40 3. Prohibition. A federally licensed firearms dealer and a federally licensed firearms
41 dealer's employee may not participate in the sale or disposition of firearms unless the

4 §505. Maintenance of records
5 1. Record of purchase. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall establish and
6 maintain a written or electronic record of purchases, sales, acquisitions and dispositions, as
7 required by 27 Code of Federal Regulations, Section 478.125. The records must, at a
8 minimum, include the make, model, caliber or gauge, manufacturer's name and serial
9 number of all firearms that the federally licensed firearms dealer acquires or disposes of no
10 later than one business day after the acquisition or disposal. Physical copies of written
11 records must be created at least monthly and stored in a secure container designed to
12 prevent loss by fire, theft or flood. Electronic records must be backed up on an external
13 server or over the Internet at the close of each business day.
14 2. Federal form required. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall retain all ATF
15 Form 4473 records on the federally licensed firearms dealer's premises, as required by 27
16 Code of Federal Regulations, Section 478.129. The records must be stored in a secure
17 container designed to prevent loss by fire, theft or flood.
18 3. Record of inventory. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall account for all
19 firearms acquired but not yet disposed of in a monthly inventory check and shall maintain
20 records of such checks in a secure location.
21 4. Record of criminal firearm trace. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall
22 maintain records of all criminal firearm traces initiated by the ATF.
23 5. Records confidential. Notwithstanding any provision of law to the contrary,
24 information contained in the records maintained under this section is confidential and may
25 not be disclosed to any person.
26 6. Inspection of records. Notwithstanding subsection 5, a federally licensed firearms
27 dealer shall permit law enforcement officers to inspect the records required to be
28 maintained under this section during regular business hours.
29 §506. Reporting
30 1. Transactions involving more than one pistol or revolver. A federally licensed
31 firearms dealer shall report a transaction involving more than one pistol or revolver to a
32 law enforcement agency, as required by 27 Code of Federal Regulations, Section 478.126a.
33 2. Thefts and losses. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall report a theft or loss
34 within 48 hours of discovery to a law enforcement agency, as required by 27 Code of
35 Federal Regulations, Section 478.39a.
36 §507. Compliance
37 1. Certification. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall, at least annually, certify
38 to the department in a form designed by the department that the federally licensed firearms
39 dealer is in compliance with the requirements of this chapter.
40 2. Inspections. A federally licensed firearms dealer shall provide the department with
41 full access to the federally licensed firearms dealer's premises for an inspection. The
42 department may conduct an inspection of a federally licensed firearms dealer's premises

14 §508. Violations
15 The following penalties apply to violations of this chapter.
16 1. First violation. A federally licensed firearms dealer who violates any provision of
17 this chapter commits a civil violation for which a fine of not less than $100 and not more
18 than $500 may be adjudged.
19 2. Subsequent violations. A federally licensed firearms dealer who violates any
20 provision of this chapter after having been adjudicated as having committed one or more
21 civil violations under this chapter or for engaging in substantially similar conduct to that
22 contained in this chapter in another jurisdiction within the previous 5-year period commits
23 a civil violation for which a fine of not less than $500 and not more than $1,000 may be
24 adjudged.

8 SUMMARY
99 This amendment replaces the bill. It removes from the bill references to the Office of
1100 Violence Prevention and clarifies the security requirements in the bill for the premises of a
1111 federally licensed firearms dealer. The amendment provides that a background check
1122 performed on an employee of a firearms dealer must be completed by an entity approved
1133 by the Department of Public Safety rather than the Federal Bureau of Investigation. The
1144 amendment creates a civil violation for false certification of required reporting data.
1155 Finally, the amendment requires the Department of Public Safety to post resources on the
1166 department's publicly accessible website, including links to authorized entities for
1177 background checks, links to authorized training materials, links to required posters to be
1188 posted at gun shows and firearm points of sale and a link to the annual compliance form.
