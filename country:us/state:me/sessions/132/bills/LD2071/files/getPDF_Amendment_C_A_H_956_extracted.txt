7 SUMMARY
88 This amendment, which is the majority report of the committee, replaces the
99 substantive provisions of the bill but retains the emergency clause and emergency
1100 preamble. The amendment allows pharmacists to prescribe, dispense or administer
1111 vaccines to a person 18 years of age or older, or a person over 6 months of age specifically
1122 for influenza and COVID-19 vaccines, and to dispense or administer other vaccines with a
1133 prescription to a person 3 years of age or older. Vaccines must be licensed by the federal
1144 Food and Drug Administration and may be recommended by the United States Centers for
1155 Disease Control and Prevention Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices or be
1166 consistent with recommendations of the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American
1177 Academy of Family Physicians or the American College of Obstetricians and
1188 Gynecologists. It removes the prohibition on pharmacy interns administering drugs and
1199 vaccines to a person under 18 years of age, which will allow pharmacy interns with
2200 appropriate training to administer a drug or vaccine under a pharmacist’s direct supervision.
2211 It removes the requirement that a pharmacist notify the person’s primary care provider
2222 within 72 hours of administering a vaccine, as this information is required to be reported
2233 in the state immunization information reporting system.
24 The amendment also clarifies that insurance carriers are authorized to provide coverage
25 for vaccines without any deductible, copayment, coinsurance or other cost-sharing
26 reimbursement that have been licensed by the federal Food and Drug Administration and
27 that are consistent with the recommendations of the American Academy of Pediatrics, the
28 American Academy of Family Physicians or the American College of Obstetricians and
29 Gynecologists.
